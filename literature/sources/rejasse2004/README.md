# Réjasse et al. (2004): independent substrate-exposure activity

[Org. Biomol. Chem. 2, 1086–1089](https://doi.org/10.1039/B4011459)
· [Metadata](metadata.json) · [Evidence](evidence.toml).

This study provides an activity measurement separate from a declining
outlet conversion. In the storage experiment (Methods, p. 1089), 25 mg
Novozym 435 is exposed to 11 mmol of either butanol or ethyl butyrate at
100 °C. Water activity is set to zero before preincubation. At the selected
age, the other substrate is added and activity is measured from a 3-minute
initial butyl-butyrate formation rate under conventional heating. All
samples in that comparison are thereby assayed at the same overall
reactant ratio; the assay does not establish the detailed damage mechanism
or distinguish irreversible molecular damage from every conditioning effect.

Figure 3 (p. 1087) uses absolute activity in µmol/(min mg of commercial
catalyst). Im4 native pixel centers for the conventional-heating circles
and triangles are retained, along with the printed fresh reference
6.3 µmol/(min mg), the reported remaining activities at 30 min (13%
butanol, 39% ethyl butyrate) and a separate reading-only allowance
0.10 µmol/(min mg). The paper does not give numerical assay uncertainty
for these markers. The microwave series is different physics and is
not used in this audit.

**Time window and logarithm are important.** Figure 4's fitted logarithmic
line contains only 0–4 min. Its intercept is 2 for the percentage
activity, implying base 10, not a natural logarithm. The printed Table 1
half-lives agree with log10(2)/k_d, confirming the conversion to a
natural exponential loss coefficient is ln(10) times the printed
coefficient. The independently observed Fig. 3 points at 10 and 30 min
test extrapolation of this initial fast-rate law.

Figures 5–6 (p. 1088) and p. 1089 report six successive runs in a
reacting 11:11 mmol ethyl-butyrate/butanol mixture at 100 °C, each run
6 hours, with recovery and rinse before reuse. Figure 5 conventional
initial production rates are read separately (Im6; 654 × 313 pixels;
reading allowance 2 µmol/min). These are operating-cycle initial
rates, not mass-normalized residual assays; recovered mass is not
reported. They demonstrate that the reacting and preincubating
environments are not interchangeable.

The chemistry, access, water activity and temperature differ from
pentyl propanoate. These results test the structure of a degradation/
conditioning law; none of the constants is a pentyl-propanoate input.
