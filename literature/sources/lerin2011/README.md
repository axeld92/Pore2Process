# Lerin et al. (2011): activity is not conversion

[Braz. J. Chem. Eng. 28, 181–188](https://doi.org/10.1590/S0104-66322011000200002)
· [Metadata](metadata.json) · [Evidence](evidence.toml).

The paper reports repeated use of commercial Novozym 435 in three
distinct batch synthesis reactions (p. 183, protocol and figure captions).
The activity assay on p. 184 measures the initial consumption of lauric
acid with n-propanol at a 3:1 molar ratio, 60 °C, 5 wt% catalyst relative
to substrates, and 15 min. The definition of residual activity is the
printed final/initial activity ratio. This is a separate chemical assay,
not calculated from the product conversion of the three use reactions.
The plotted values are retained as printed residual activity; do not
multiply successive values or assume a mechanistic fresh-fraction state.

The enzyme is washed with n-hexane, dried for one hour at 40 °C and
rested for 24 h after each use; a 0.2 g aliquot is allocated to activity
assays and the remaining catalyst reused. The changing recovered mass
and any adjustment of substrate amount are not tabulated. Cumulative
reaction-contact hours therefore must not be relabelled continuous
immersion time or fixed inventory.

Figures 1–3 have paired independently measured residual activity and
conversion means (standard error bars and Tukey group letters at p<0.05).
Native source image marker center y positions are stored, with axis
coordinates, reaction condition and product for each figure. The
readings are approximate. The reading allowance of 1 percentage point
per marker is not the experimental SEM. Fig. 1 includes approximate
visible cap half-heights; the small or overlapping bars in Fig. 2 and 3
do not supply reliable numerical SEMs at this reading resolution.

- Fig. 1 (p. 185, Im1): open diamond conversion, filled diamond activity.
- Fig. 2 (p. 185, Im2): open square conversion, filled square activity.
  Only eight cycles are plotted, although the general Methods say ten.
  Cycles 9 and 10 are unavailable, not zero or extrapolated.
- Fig. 3 (p. 186, Im3): filled diamond conversion, open square activity.
  Conversion after the first cycle has the same reported Tukey group
  letter; the independent assay undergoes much larger changes. Some
  measured activity ratios exceed 100%; retain the observations, not a
  clipped or enforced monotone state.

The different media, intermittent recovery and non-target chemistry make
this a test of interpretation and model structure, not an independently
measured deactivation rate for continuous pentyl propanoate production.
