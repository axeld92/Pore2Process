# Sbardelotto et al. (2018): geranyl butanoate

[Article](https://doi.org/10.21577/0100-4042.20170259),
*Química Nova* 41, 905–911 (open article; [journal page](https://quimicanova.sbq.org.br/detalhe_artigo.asp?id=6812)).

Figure 3 panel A2 (p. 909): Novozym 435 only (not the homemade
polyurethane preparation), 70 °C, 150 rpm, geraniol:butanoic acid
5:1 mol/mol, molecular sieves 20 mg/mL. Catalyst dosage is
relative to total substrate mass (p. 906). The sampled
conversion marks, rounded to whole percentage points, are in
[`loading_progress.csv`](loading_progress.csv). The figure uses
equally spaced categorical time labels, so use the printed times
and not horizontal pixel distances. In particular 0.17 h is
10.2 min as printed, not the 5 min cited for the specific rate
in the text (Methods also list a different start time). Retain
this discrepancy. The first reading has partial overlap with
the specific-rate series; the stated margins are figure-reading
scenarios, not experimental error bars. At 70–80% conversion
all catalytic curves cross between the first two sampled times;
the close times do not depend on comparing final conversion
near 100%. No mixture volume/density is reported.

The separately plotted zero-catalyst conversion is not a
positive loading and is excluded from log-loading comparisons;
it is small at the two early times but not zero. The authors'
Eq. 1 reports cumulative mass converted per catalyst mass per
elapsed time. It is an average productivity, not an
instantaneous independent rate measurement; do not use it as an
additional kinetic observation. Water scavenging distinguishes
this chemistry from the pentyl propanoate batches.
