# Damstrup et al. (2007): wet filling and continuous glycerolysis

[J. Agric. Food Chem. 55, 7786–7792](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf063366p).
User-supplied PDF in the private inbox; [metadata](metadata.json).
[Evidence](evidence.toml) keeps the values from the independent experiments separate.

- The catalyst is commercial Novozym 435 on acrylic Lewatit VP OC 1600,
  with reported dry bulk density 430 kg/m³ and bead size range 300–900 µm
  (Materials, p. 7787). Chemistry: glycerolysis of sunflower oil in a
  tertiary-alcohol solvent, not short-chain-acid esterification.
- Figure 2 (p. 7788): 4, 8 and 12 g dry charges in the standard 15 mm ID,
  200 mm column, at 40 °C and the stated feed. The ordinate is MAG weight
  percent of the analyzed product mixture, not acid conversion. The abscissa
  uses Eq. 1, `tau = W/(rho_bulk,dry Q)`, a catalyst-based nominal time,
  not a measured liquid RTD. Thus at equal nominal time W/Q is equal, but
  Q and filling degree vary. The plotted times are retained from marker
  coordinates rather than forced onto an equally spaced nominal grid.
  The paper gives only the overall flow range for the figure, not a
  paired flow ledger; Eq. 1 would imply flows outside that range at some
  short-time/high-load points. Reconstructed Q values are not measurements.
- Marker readings: original embedded Im2 (788 × 471 pixels), upper-left
  origin. Filled square = 4 g, open square = 8 g, triangle = 12 g.
  Zero-time overlapping markers are omitted from the comparisons.
  Error bars show SD of duplicate determinations; exact numerical bars
  are not tabulated. Reading-only allowances of 0.3 min and 1 MAG weight
  percentage point are used as resolution scenarios, not experimental SD.
- Table 1 (p. 7788) supplies independent dimensions, loadings, nominal
  times, flow rates, MAG means and SDs. The five 220 kg/m³, 20 min rows
  form a separate scale-up comparison. Its slightly different feed
  specification is retained and its 227 kg/m³ rows are not silently
  substituted for Figure 2 points.
- Table 2 (p. 7789) gives unconfined dry/wetted *layer* volumes and
  expansion at 0.5, 1 and 24 h (triplicate mean and SD). The expansion
  is a ratio of bulk layer volumes; it is neither measured porosity nor
  particle volume. Comparing the free-wet layer to the available column
  volume can identify underfill or a nominal overfill/constraint, not
  the in situ structure or active fraction. The printed dry filling
  percentages and the independently calculated ones are kept apart.
- Figure 4/Table 3 (pp. 7789–7790): the age, cumulative exposure and
  reported relative MAG content concern long-term continuous production.
  The latter is explicitly *calculated from MAG content*, not measured
  remaining catalyst activity. Do not use 55% or 90% as an independently
  measured activity state. The prose's 1200 h half-life would give 2400 h
  to 25% under Eq. 5, rather than the stated 2200 h; these are author
  projections, not a validated catalyst replacement schedule.
- Table 4 (p. 7790) gives the authors' calculated average MAG formation
  rates for two particle-size fractions. At short nominal exposure the
  smaller particles give greater mean rates. These rates are not direct
  fresh specific-activity assays.

Use these data to audit whether nominal catalyst contact time alone
explains the measured PBR performance, not to transfer a fitted glycerolysis
rate, swelling factor, or catalyst-aging law to pentyl propanoate.
