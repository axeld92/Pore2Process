# Kuperkar et al. (2014): isobutyl propionate

[Article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcatb.2013.10.024), *Journal of
Molecular Catalysis B: Enzymatic* 99, 143–149. Read from a publicly
accessible [article copy](https://file1.lookchem.com/doi/2022/1/27/cbf3ff7c-fb4e-49cd-84e8-e6c6cd58a84a.pdf)
(hash in metadata).

Figure 2A/B and §3.1.2 (p. 146), Novozym 435, neat propionic
acid/isobutanol, 40 °C, 200 rpm, nominal 1:1 molar ratio
(0.1 mol each, Methods p. 144; other parameters kept fixed).
[`loading_progress.csv`](loading_progress.csv) digitizes Figure 2A
markers, not the connecting smooth curves. Read to whole conversion
percentage points; margins of 1–2 pp describe figure reading only.
The 2.5% trace has no separately resolved 0.5 h marker. The
paper reports 82, 75 and 72% late conversions at 5, 7.5 and 10%,
consistent with the plot. The 7.5% and 10% curves approach different
late conversions, suggesting more than simple rescaling of time.

The paper labels the loadings “w/w” without specifying the
denominator clearly enough for harmonised absolute loading.
The relative mass ratios within this series are still meaningful.
Do not infer g/L or include this series in a common absolute
mass-ratio saturation fit. Figure 2B reports initial rates per
gram of enzyme (and ambiguous absolute units); they are not
total rates and are not used to calibrate a mass-dependent
total-rate law. The authors' proposed agglomeration and mixing
mechanisms remain hypotheses.
