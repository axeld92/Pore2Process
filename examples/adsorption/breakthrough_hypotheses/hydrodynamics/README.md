# Confined carrier flow and clean-carbon tracer

The test asks how much of the carrier can travel in the outer half-particle diameter of an 11 mm adsorption tube and how often that liquid returns to the core or reaches a grain. The synthetic domain has 747 nonoverlapping 0.80 mm spheres, an axial period of 3.67756 mm and a continuous porosity of 0.427. The tube radius, bed voidage, flow rates and the mass implied by the printed packed density come from the Alhamed comparison. Applying 740 kg/m³ to the 25 cm bed gives 17.58114 g dry carbon. The synthetic spheres start from a densely packed template and undergo seeded displacement. Equivalent spheres and axial periodicity give a short high-channeling scenario with defined geometry. [Alhamed Table 1 and Figure 5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhazmat.2009.05.002)

At 23.3 and 62.5 mL/min the superficial particle Reynolds numbers are 3.92 and 10.52. The interstitial particle Péclet numbers are 7,656 and 20,536 using the project's water and phenol transport inputs of 1000 kg/m³, 0.8333 mPa s and \(D=10^{-9}\) m²/s. These transport values are scenario inputs carried over from the film comparison. The MRI reference uses a 21 mm bore and 3 mm particles, and the independently checked cross-sectional flow shares are reported on that geometry. The MRI flow shares therefore give evidence about possible wall channeling in a different column. [Water-flow MRI reference](https://doi.org/10.1002/aic.18322)

## Simulation and checks

- The external application follows the stock OpenLB mesh, geometry, lattice initialization and simulation sequence. It uses D3Q27 TRT, Kupershtokh forcing, stock sphere and cylinder indicators and bounce-back boundaries on both carbon and the tube. The body force is axial. The resolved pore area sets the axial throughput target. A native run needs throughput error at most 0.2%, Mach at most 0.04, density departure at most 0.002, and steady velocity and kinetic energy before export. The OpenLB vendor manifest is checked at build time and after each native run.
- Julia projects the shared-face fluxes onto local conservation with the native axial throughput as a constraint. Live pore voxels have six-face connectivity. Corner-only pore pockets below 0.1% of liquid volume remain counted in native porosity and are quantified separately. The existing 5% local face-correction screen remains part of qualification. Radial flow uses signed axial flux with square-face overlap at each cut, averaged over all axial planes. Carbon fractions use continuous sphere surface and volume inside the shell. Liquid fractions use the complementary continuous volume.
- Every inert marker also records its first contact with carbon. Its numerical track reflects from the outer tube and from the artificial axial cuts during molecular diffusion. Advection accounts for downstream arrivals and upstream returns separately. The clean-carbon result counts any first grain encounter as complete capture. This gives a short-period idealized contact test; it supplies no adsorption parameters. The finer step is 0.5 ms, half the comparison step. Reflux, recovery, mean transit, time-step sensitivity, and grid sensitivity are reported independently. Wall/core exchange is defined by the inlet and eventual outlet position of recovered markers across one 3.67756 mm section.
- A shell flow or probability difference of 0.02 between grids, a mean transit difference of 5% and a transit-variance difference of 10% are the engineering screens used for this synthetic case. Mass recovery includes both outlet and inlet returns. The common hydraulic cut is picked by low reverse throughput before the tracer check. Carbon geometry and quantities passed downstream are frozen before scoring any column outcome.

## Handoff and units

`field.f64` contains little-endian Float64 records of zero-based voxel indices, three velocity components in m/s and density in kg/m³. The axial cell centre is \((i+1/2)h\), increasing with flow. In both transverse directions the axis-relative cell centre is \((j+1/2-N_\perp/2)h\). Use `request.json` for the geometry and forcing and `verified_field.json` for the throughput and lattice diagnostics. `radial.json` records the native-to-face correction and the cut-dependent flow in m³/s. `tracer/summary.json` gives transit and contact times in seconds, local reflection lengths in metres and normalized counts by tracer fate. The reduced column handoff has a wall and core transition matrix with rows indexed by the inlet region and columns by the outlet region. It carries a qualification flag and hashes for the physical inputs, fields and paths used.

The 11 fields in each little-endian Float64 marker record are entry region, last region, exit direction, elapsed time, first carbon-contact time, first carbon-contact region, first tube-contact time, carbon reflection length in the shell, carbon reflection length in the core, residence time in the shell, and residence time in the shell before first carbon contact. Region codes 1 and 2 denote wall and core. A contact region of zero and a binary NaN timestamp indicate no contact. Exit codes 1, −1 and 0 indicate outlet, inlet return and time-censored.

At 23.3 mL/min, the half-particle shell occupies 14.02% of the tube cross section and has 22.59% of its liquid, 7.63% of the carbon mass and 8.24% of the grain surface. Its continuous local voidage is 68.8%. The eight- and eleven-cell-per-particle meshes carried 47.52% and 45.82% of the flow in this shell. Tracer inlet-to-outlet exchange probabilities were 8–9% per 3.68 mm period. On the finer mesh, the mean transit was 0.20 s for markers entering the shell and 0.54 s for markers entering the core. An ideal first encounter with carbon intercepted 12.28% of the shell-entry dose and 53.45% of the core-entry dose before downstream arrival. All 16,384 inert markers exited downstream on that mesh.

The low-rate grid comparison passed the flow-share, mean-transit and contact-probability limits. The exit-time variance changed by 14%, above the 10% limit. The local face correction fell from 19.81% to 11.18%, both above 5%. For raw and corrected face fluxes, the shell flow estimates differ by at most 0.36 percentage points. Averaging the native cell velocities directly gives a shell share of 44.11% on either low-rate grid. The shell's share of total marker residence time was 22.63% on the finer mesh, close to its 22.59% share of continuous void volume. The simulated shell is unusually open compared with the independently sourced radial shape scenarios for an 11 mm bore. These results bound a high-channeling case and support an exploratory column comparison.

At 62.5 mL/min the shell carries 49.19% of the corrected face flow on the coarser grid and 47.11% on the finer grid. The native cell average gives 46.11% and 45.52%. The finer-grid mean outlet transit is 0.1428 s against 0.1432 s from liquid volume divided by flow. Shell-entry and core-entry means were 0.077 s and 0.203 s. Of 16,384 inert markers, 16,380 exited downstream, three returned to the inlet and one was censored. One-period clean-carbon escape was 75.96%, with 91.28% escape for the shell-entry dose and 61.79% for the core-entry dose. The exchange probabilities were about 12% in each direction per period. Times and clean-contact outcomes passed the time-step screens. The largest shell flow change between meshes was 2.32 points, the variance change was 17.4% and local face correction fell from 17.84% to 10.30%. Those three checks exceed the numerical qualification limits.

The four primary hydraulic fields passed the native gates, and shared-face projection conserved flow at each cross section to better than \(10^{-12}\) relative. Independent geometry sampling and a plug-flow test also passed. The wall and core splits give a physically interpretable high-channeling condition for fixed-mass column sensitivity. The local handoff and transit variance are unqualified for a pore-resolved breakthrough prediction. The 3.68 mm synthetic period gives no direct information on the actual packing structure or flow distributors in the paper's 25 cm bed. [Independent radial benchmark](../benchmark/README.md)

In the 23.3 mL/min coarse-grid numerical control, use of the high-rate TRT magic of 0.04 and a shorter step changed the corrected shell share from 47.52% to 50.55%. The native estimate changed from 44.11% to 47.75%. The change from these settings at the same flow is larger than the originally observed difference between rates on the coarse mesh. Both settings produce a high-channeling scenario. The exact rate dependence remains sensitive to the lattice settings. The control passed the native gates at Mach 0.03742 and was used only for hydraulic sensitivity.

## Commands

From the repository root, use the installed OpenLB 1.9r0 vendor tree, project Python and bootstrapped Julia. The runs below were prepared on macOS arm64 using Python 3.12.9, GCC 15.2.0 and Julia 1.11.6.

```sh
source scripts/env.sh
module=examples.adsorption.breakthrough_hypotheses.hydrodynamics
uv run python -m "$module.native" --build
base="$(uv run python -m "$module.native" --prepare)"
uv run python -m "$module.native" --run q233 --base "$base" --mesh 8 --tau-delta .013
uv run python -m "$module.native" --run q233 --base "$base" --mesh 8 --tau-delta .0048 --warm q233_N8 --tag _lowMa
uv run python -m "$module.native" --run q233 --base "$base" --mesh 11 --tau-delta .0062 --warm q233_N8_lowMa
uv run python -m "$module.native" --run q625 --base "$base" --mesh 8 --tau-delta .002 --magic .04 --warm q233_N8_lowMa --tag _m04
uv run python -m "$module.native" --run q625 --base "$base" --mesh 8 --tau-delta .0016 --magic .04 --warm q625_N8_m04 --tag _m04l
uv run python -m "$module.native" --run q625 --base "$base" --mesh 11 --tau-delta .0022 --magic .04 --warm q625_N8_m04 --tag _m04l
uv run python -m "$module.handoff" "$base" q233_N8_lowMa q233_N11 q625_N8_m04l q625_N11_m04l
uv run python -m "$module.tracer" "$base" q233_N8_lowMa q233_N11 q625_N8_m04l q625_N11_m04l --cut-fraction .972972972972973
uv run python -m "$module.report" "$base" "$base/column_handoff.json" q233_N8_lowMa q233_N11 q625_N8_m04l q625_N11_m04l
uv run python -m "$module.native" --run q233 --base "$base" --mesh 8 --tau-delta .0048 --magic .04 --warm q625_N8_m04l --tag _m04control
uv run python -m "$module.native" --run q233 --base "$base" --mesh 8 --tau-delta .004 --magic .04 --warm q233_N8_m04control --tag _m04control_lowMa
uv run python -m "$module.handoff" "$base" q233_N8_m04control_lowMa
uv run python -m "$module.report" "$base" "$base/reference_summary.json" q233_N8_lowMa q233_N11 q625_N8_m04l q625_N11_m04l --compact --control q233_N8_m04control_lowMa
uv run python -m "$module.verify"
```

The first q233 run, the first q625 run and the q233 `_m04control` run create warm fields. Each exits at the Mach check after writing its field. Execute the subsequent lines once those fields exist. The initial q625 runs with stock TRT magic 0.1875 produced nonfinite flow at both tested time steps. The alternate numerical treatment uses the same stock TRT collision with a lower odd/even product and fixed physical viscosity, flow and packing. Case directories retain the exact parameters and build hashes. The standalone verification samples the shell integrals independently and tests the distinction between tube and carbon contact on a plug-flow voxel.
