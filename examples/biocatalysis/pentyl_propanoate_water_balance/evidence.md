# Evidence for the matched-conversion water comparison

## Reactor inventories

Lorenzo and Ortega report a 50 mL stirred vessel, a 40 mL initial charge,
a condenser, and the addition of propanoic acid after bringing the
pentanol and catalyst to temperature. The selected 2:1, 10% catalyst
batches used 29.7 mL of pentanol and 10.3 mL of acid. Table S1 gives the
acid density used to calculate the charge on a 25 °C basis. Figure 1 and
the methods describe the cooler as limiting evaporation. The nominal
10 mL vapor volume covers the space above the initial liquid in the
vessel. Condenser hold-up, net water loss, initial water of the charge
and the batch bead-film coefficient remain unavailable.
[Lorenzo and Ortega (2026), Section 2, Figure 1 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.6c02375) ·
[Supporting Information, Tables S1–S2](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/_/33164325)

All 75 selected post-start rows in Table S2 report equal water and ester
mole fractions at the printed precision. The authors estimated water by
mass balance after analyzing the organic constituents. These values
record the implied water on the batch reaction path. Independent water
activity and liquid-to-solid water storage measurements remain unavailable.
[Retained batch compositions and extraction](../../../literature/sources/lorenzo2026/README.md)

The continuous study used a 6.0 g catalyst inventory and molecular-sieve
treated feed. It describes Karl Fischer measurements during reagent
preparation. Actual residual water in the 2:1 feed and catalyst hydration
during the column runs are unavailable. The study estimates wet bed
voidage from tests with water. The film screening borrows this voidage
and the reported bead diameter from the prior reactor reconstruction.
[Lorenzo et al. (2025), Sections 2.1–2.3 and Appendix A](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.5c02780)

Water vapor pressure uses the reference saturation correlation in IAPWS
SR1-86 Eq. 1. The calculation assumes ideal vapor in a finite, closed
headspace, with all of that volume at the reaction temperature.
[IAPWS saturation release](https://www.iapws.org/relguide/Supp-sat.html)

Melgosa et al. measured **0.7 ± 0.2 g water per 100 g** of a separate
Lipozyme 435 lot before treatment. They extracted with dry methanol
for 24 h and used Karl Fischer titration. The complete-release
sensitivity uses 0.9 g/100 g as a scenario. The published spread
belongs to that catalyst sample. The value gives an initial water
inventory scale and provides no sorption isotherm for the reacting
mixture. [Melgosa et al. (2015), Section 2.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.supflu.2014.11.003)

## Water activity versus liquid composition

Smith's Table B.3 reports experimental binary water–pentanol VLE at
101.3 kPa. The four selected pentanol-rich points are at 378.42–388.58 K.
The table reports the average liquid and vapor mole fractions and
water activity coefficients. Calculating $a_w=x_w\gamma_w$ gives
0.371–0.642 for $x_w=0.0819$–0.1852. The independent ideal-vapor
calculation $y_w P/p_w^*$ differs by at most 0.009 from the reported
coefficient-based activities. The reported liquid repeat differences
are 0.0059–0.1828 in mole fraction. A sensitivity keeps the two points
with repeat differences at most 0.03. The dry limit supplies the
origin, and the curves interpolate between available points. The
table was transcribed from the archived PDF text and checked against
its published vapor compositions. The hot binary curves give
composition sensitivity for a neat-pentanol surrogate. The absolute
activities of the esterification liquid at 40–80 °C remain unresolved.
[Smith (2017), Table B.3 and Section 5.4](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/188223613.pdf)

Amamehi et al. report eight liquid–liquid tie lines at each of 30 °C
and 50 °C for propanoic acid, water and pentanol at atmospheric
pressure. Reported standard uncertainties are 0.0003 in mole fraction,
0.1 K and 1 kPa. Across both tables, the organic-rich phases contain
about 0.176–0.232 mole fraction water and the water-rich partners
contain about 0.953–0.990. The water chemical potential is equal on a
tie line. The projection estimates that common activity from the
water-rich composition treated as nearly ideal and retains the
observed composition of both phases. Lorenzo and Ortega's water–ester
calorimetry indicates limited miscibility at 25 and 50 °C. An
ester-excluded projection and an ester-as-pentanol sensitivity
probe this unresolved fourth component. The latter is an assumed
change to the solvent count and has no assigned probability.
[Amamehi et al. (2024), Tables 1–2](https://doi.org/10.12962/j2964710X.v5i1.19035) ·
[Lorenzo and Ortega (2026), Section 5 and Figure 9](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.6c02375)

## Water transport and rate sensitivity

Su et al. report **$(5.2\pm0.8)\times10^{-10}$ m²/s** for water in
pentanol at **22 ± 1 °C**, inferred from water microdroplet dissolution.
The screen scales the measurement with $T/\mu$ using measured
pentanol viscosity from the earlier film audit. This describes a
temperature-scaled single-solvent analogue. Concentration-dependent
multicomponent diffusion at the operating temperatures remains
unavailable. The packed-bed coefficient uses the same
Wilson–Geankoplis correlation and surface-area convention as the film
audit. An isolated stagnant sphere is a weaker-transfer sensitivity.
[Su et al. (2010), Sections II–III and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3298857) ·
[Earlier film sources](../pentyl_propanoate_film_water/film/evidence.md)

The illustrative rate sensitivity uses independent measured
Novozym 435 initial rates at four controlled activities in MTBE.
The underlying butyric-acid/ethanol reaction and catalyst conditioning
differ from the pentanol reactor. The earlier check also found poor
transfer of the magnitude between independent MTBE studies. Use
relative rate only inside the measured interval and treat it as
context for the activity difference.
[Tjørnelund et al. (2023), Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1002/cctc.202201207) ·
[Earlier controlled-batch comparison](../pentyl_propanoate_film_water/REPORT.md)
